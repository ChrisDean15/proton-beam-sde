  This evaluation provides a complete representation of the        725 1451   18
nuclear data needed for transport, damage, heating,                725 1451   19
radioactivity, and shielding applications over the incident        725 1451   20
proton energy range from 1 to 150 MeV.  The evaluation utilizes    725 1451   21
MF=6, MT=5 to represent all reaction data.  Production cross       725 1451   22
sections and emission spectra are given for neutrons, protons,     725 1451   23
deuterons, tritons, alpha particles, gamma rays, and all           725 1451   24
residual nuclides produced (A>5) in the reaction chains. To        725 1451   25
summarize, the ENDF sections with non-zero data above are:         725 1451   26
                                                                   725 1451   27
  MF=3 MT=  2  Integral of nuclear plus interference components    725 1451   28
               of the elastic scattering cross section             725 1451   29
                                                                   725 1451   30
       MT=  5  Sum of binary (p,n') and (p,x) reactions            725 1451   31
                                                                   725 1451   32
  MF=6 MT=  2  Elastic (p,p) angular distributions given as        725 1451   33
               ratios of the differential nuclear-plus-            725 1451   34
               interference to the integrated value.               725 1451   35
                                                                   725 1451   36
       MT=  5  Production cross sections and energy-angle          725 1451   37
               distributions for emission neutrons, protons,       725 1451   38
               deuterons, and alphas; and angle-integrated         725 1451   39
               spectra for gamma rays and residual nuclei that     725 1451   40
               are stable against particle emission                725 1451   41

  The evaluation is based on nuclear model calculations that       725 1451   43
have been benchmarked to experimental data, especially for n +     725 1451   44
14N and p + 14N reactions (Ch97). We use the GNASH code system     725 1451   45
(Yo92), which utilizes Hauser-Feshbach statistical,                725 1451   46
preequilibrium and direct-reaction theories. Spherical optical     725 1451   47
model calculations are used to obtain particle transmission        725 1451   48
coefficients for the Hauser-Feshbach calculations, as well as      725 1451   49
for the elastic proton angular distributions.                      725 1451   50
  Cross sections and spectra for producing individual residual     725 1451   51
nuclei are included for reactions. The energy-angle-correlations   725 1451   52
for all outgoing particles are based on Kalbach systematics        725 1451   53
(Ka88).                                                            725 1451   54
  A model was developed to calculate the energy distributions of   725 1451   55
all recoil nuclei in the GNASH calculations (Ch96). The recoil     725 1451   56
energy distributions are represented in the laboratory system in   725 1451   57
MT=5, MF=6, and are given as isotropic in the lab system. All      725 1451   58
other data in MT=5,MF=6 are given in the center-of-mass system.    725 1451   59
This method of representation utilizes the LCT=3 option approved   725 1451   60
at the November, 1996, CSEWG meeting.                              725 1451   61
  Preequilibrium corrections were performed in the course of the   725 1451   62
GNASH calculations using the exciton model of Kalbach (Ka77,       725 1451   63
Ka85), validated by comparison with calculations using Feshbach,   725 1451   64
Kerman, Koonin (FKK) theory [Ch93].  Discrete level data from      725 1451   65
nuclear data sheets were matched to continuum level densities      725 1451   66
using the formulation of Ignatyuk (Ig75) and pairing and shell     725 1451   67
parameters from the Cook (Co67) analysis. Neutron and charged-     725 1451   68
particle transmission coefficients were obtained from the          725 1451   69
optical potentials, as discussed below.  Gamma-ray transmission    725 1451   70
coefficients were calculated using the Kopecky-Uhl model (Ko90).   725 1451   71

       DETAILS OF THE p + N-14 ANALYSIS                            725 1451   73
                                                                   725 1451   74
 GNASH calculations [Yo92] were performed for neutron and proton   725 1451   75
reactions on nitrogen up to 150 MeV, and the calculated results    725 1451   76
were benchmarked against experimental data. The present            725 1451   77
evaluation made extensive use of our previous work [Ch96a,Ch97].   725 1451   78
Very minor differences with this earlier work exist due to         725 1451   79
recent developments in the GNASH code.  Additionally, the          725 1451   80
(angle-integrated) emission spectra of heavy recoils were          725 1451   81
calculated using our model described in Ref. [Ch96].  Some         725 1451   82
additional information on this evaluation can be found in Ref.     725 1451   83
[Ch97].                                                            725 1451   84
 Between 20 and 150 MeV, the optical models used (the neutron      725 1451   85
potential of Islam below 60 MeV [Is88]; Madland's potential        725 1451   86
[Ma88] at higher energies, with Lane transformations for the       725 1451   87
proton potential) provided a reasonably good description of        725 1451   88
measured reaction cross section data. But since a very accurate    725 1451   89
description of the reaction cross section is important for         725 1451   90
determining secondary particle spectra, we slightly modify the     725 1451   91
calculated results to better describe the experimental data, and   725 1451   92
renormalize the calculated transmission coefficients               725 1451   93
accordingly. Experimental proton-induced nonelastic cross          725 1451   94
sections from the Bauhoff compilation [Ba86] were used for this    725 1451   95
purpose. The SCAT2 code [Be92] was used to calculate the           725 1451   96
transmission coefficients. No measurements for the neutron         725 1451   97
reaction cross section on nitrogen exist above about 50 MeV.       725 1451   98
However, systematics have been determined from a number of         725 1451   99
target elements at 95 MeV by DeJuren [De50], and for 100 MeV       725 1451  100
protons by Kirby and Link [Ki66] (at this energy the proton and    725 1451  101
neutron reaction cross sections would be expected to be very       725 1451  102
similar). We have, therefore, used these systematics to guide      725 1451  103
our evaluated reaction cross sections. Additionally, below 50      725 1451  104
MeV we have also been guided by the proton-induced reaction        725 1451  105
cross sections of Carlson et al. [Ca75]. Experimental total        725 1451  106
elastic scattering values of Islam et al. [Is88], Olsson et al.    725 1451  107
[Ol90], and Petler et al. [Pe85] were obtained by subtracting      725 1451  108
their angle-integrated elastic data from the evaluated total       725 1451  109
cross sections (see below).                                        725 1451  110
  Preequilibrium spectra were calculated with the exciton model    725 1451  111
in the GNASH code [Ka77,Ka85].  Nuclear level densities were       725 1451  112
determined using the Ignatyuk model [Ig75], as implemented by      725 1451  113
Arthur et al.  [Yo92]. Pairing energies were obtained from the     725 1451  114
Cook systematics with the Los Alamos extensions to light nuclei    725 1451  115
from [Ar83].  This continuum level density formulation is          725 1451  116
matched continuously onto discrete low-lying levels at the lower   725 1451  117
excitation energies.  Discrete level information (energy, spin,    725 1451  118
parity, gamma-ray branching ratios) is tabulated for each          725 1451  119
nuclide in an input file, which is based on the Ajzenberg-Selove   725 1451  120
compilations.  For each nucleus we performed a level-density       725 1451  121
analysis and determined the excitation energy at which we judged   725 1451  122
the level data complete. Gamma-ray transmission coefficients       725 1451  123
were obtained from the Kopecky-Uhl model [Ko92].                   725 1451  124
  Comparison was made with new (unpublished) data from South       725 1451  125
Africa (Cowley et al.) for 150 and 200 MeV protons on 14N. The     725 1451  126
evaluated proton emission spectra were in reasonable agreement     725 1451  127
with the experimental data. For deuteron and alpha ejectiles,      725 1451  128
the quality of agreement was poorer.  However, modeling cluster    725 1451  129
emission in these nuclei is difficult, and the cross sections      725 1451  130
are small, so the practical impact is low.                         725 1451  131
